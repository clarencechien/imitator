Autonomy and Innovation自主與創新

Monday, August 24, 20262026年8月24日 星期一

Listen to Podcast

Listen to this post:聆聽此篇文章：

Log in to listen

While not every Western followed the cliché, by the 1930s cowboy serials had landed on a consistent visual cue: the hero of the show wore a white hat, and the villain wore a black one. At the end of the day, however, they both were cowboys with cowboy hats.雖然並非每部西部片都如此，但到 1930 年代，牛仔連續劇已形成一致的視覺暗示：主角穿白帽，反派穿黑帽。但歸根究底，他們都是戴著牛仔帽的牛仔。

Westerns aren’t much of a cultural touchpoint anymore, but the “white hat” and “black hat” nomenclature is very relevant in tech: hackers who are focused on patching vulnerabilities and protecting software are “white hat hackers”, while hackers who are focused on exploiting vulnerabilities for malicious reasons are “black hat hackers”. Of course this can very quickly become complicated: governments might employ hackers to break into enemy software installations — are they white hats or black hats? Or consider bug bounty programs, wherein large software companies pay bug bounties to hackers who find and report vulnerabilities; it’s basically using money to incentivize would-be black hat hackers to be white hat hackers.西部片已不再是文化焦點，但「白帽」與「黑帽」的稱呼在科技界非常通用：專注於修補漏洞與保護軟體的駭客稱為「白帽駭客」，而以惡意目的利用漏洞的則稱為「黑帽駭客」。當然，情況可能變得複雜：政府雇傭駭客入侵敵方軟體，他們算白帽還是黑帽？或者考慮漏洞獎金計畫，大公司支付獎金給發現並回報漏洞的駭客，這基本上是用金錢誘使潛在的黑帽駭客轉為白帽駭客。

The actual takeaway is that all of this complexity is overwrought: just as a cowboy is a cowboy, a hacker is a hacker; the hat is not a statement of capability, but rather intentions, and those intentions are shaped by incentives. The best way to attack infrastructure is to find a vulnerability and exploit it; the best way to defend infrastructure is to find a vulnerability and patch it. It’s all the same skillset.真正的重點在於這些複雜度都過於冗贅：就像牛仔就是牛仔，駭客就是駭客；帽子代表的是意圖而非能力，而意圖受激勵機制影響。攻擊與防禦基礎設施的最佳方式都是尋找漏洞並採取行動（利用或修補），這所需的技能集完全相同。

This delineation between capability and intent and incentive is critical when it comes to AI. At the end of last month’s Article Who’s Afraid of Chinese Models, I discussed a mysterious attack that model host Hugging Face had just endured, which they were only able to fight off with the help of open weight Chinese models, and wrote:在 AI 領域中，能力、意圖與誘因之間的區分至關重要。在上個月的《誰在害怕中國模型》一文末尾，我討論了模型託管平台 Hugging Face 剛經歷的一場神秘攻擊，而他們僅在開源權重中國模型的幫助下才得以擊退，當時我寫道：

It’s difficult to overstate how wrong-headed the Trump administration’s panicked response to Anthropic’s release of Fable was, particularly since it exacerbated Anthropic’s worst tendencies in terms of assuming only they can be trusted with powerful AI. In a world with only one AI, it might make sense to reserve the most powerful cybersecurity capabilities for the U.S. government and trusted allies; however, that’s not the world we live in.川普政府對 Anthropic 發布 Fable 的恐慌反應極其錯誤，尤其是這加劇了 Anthropic 認為只有他們能被信任來處理強大 AI 的傾向。在只有一個 AI 的世界中，將最強的網路安全能力保留給美國政府及其盟友或許合理，但現實並非如此。

There are and will be models eminently capable of mounting cybersecurity attacks on existing infrastructure, and those models will be — already are — widely available. The best defense — the only viable defense, in fact — will be to make sure defenders have access to the best models as well. Right now defenders are effectively banned from using Fable or Sol for cybersecurity because of Trump administration directives; that means the best alternative is using models from a country which has been trying to weaken our cyber defenses for years. This is insane!目前已有且將會有極強能力的模型可用於對現有基礎設施發動網路攻擊。最好的防禦方式是確保防禦者也能使用頂尖模型。但目前川普政府的指令禁止防禦者使用 Fable 或 Sol，這導致他們只能使用來自長期削弱我方防禦之國家的模型。這太瘋狂了！

The point is the one I made in the introduction: when it comes to cybersecurity, the capability that is necessary for good defense is the exact same capability that is necessary for good offense; the color of the hat is a matter of who is actually prompting the AI.重點在於我在引言中提到的：在網路安全方面，良好防禦所需的能力與良好攻擊所需的能力完全相同；至於是哪種帽子，則取決於誰在引導 AI。

And, sometimes, not even that is clear: it turns out that the entity that hacked Hugging Face was actually OpenAI, as a series of unconstrained agents being evaluated for their cybersecurity capabilities found and exploited a bug in the package manager in their sandbox; that package manager had Internet access and a sufficiently writeable file system such that the agents could communicate with each other over time. The entire chain of vulnerability discovery and exploit creation culminated in the so-called “Hugging Face incident”.有時甚至連這點都不明確：事實證明駭入 Hugging Face 的實體其實是 OpenAI，因為一系列被評估網路安全能力的無約束代理程式發現並利用了沙盒中套件管理器的漏洞；該管理器具有網路存取權限和足夠的可寫入檔案系統，使代理程式能隨時間相互溝通。這一連串的漏洞發現與漏洞利用過程最終導致了所謂的「Hugging Face 事件」。

The Hugging Face IncidentHugging Face 事件

There is an entire Article to be written about the implications of this specific incident and what it says about AI risk; some of my takeaways are still up in the air pending OpenAI’s promised release of an in-depth technical report (my preliminary takeaway is that the agents were not “cheating” but rather doing what they were told to do; of course that’s arguably even scarier). The part I want to focus on today, however, came at the end of a presentation OpenAI’s Eric Wallace and Michael Dalton made at the Black Hat USA conference about the Hugging Face incident. This was Dalton summarizing Lessons Learned:關於這次特定事件的影響及其對 AI 風險的啟示，值得撰寫一篇完整的文章；我的一些結論仍待 OpenAI 承諾發布的深入技術報告後才能確定（我的初步看法是，代理程式並非在「作弊」，而是在執行指令；這反而可能更可怕）。然而，我今天想關注的部分，出現在 OpenAI 的 Eric Wallace 和 Michael Dalton 在 Black Hat USA 大會上針對 Hugging Face 事件所做的簡報末尾。以下是 Dalton 對經驗教訓的總結：

We have seen what will be a dramatic acceleration of offensive capability for attackers. We have an existence proof that was unintentional, but it exists before us, and we have as a consequence seen a glimpse into the near future of what attacks will look like for our industry. The challenge is that we need a similar acceleration of defense. Today we see fully automated offence as possible, but we have no such existence proof for full automation of core defensive loops and cycles in behavior.我們看到了攻擊者攻擊能力的劇烈加速。雖然這是一個非預期的存在證明，但它讓我們得以窺見產業攻擊模式的近未來。挑戰在於我們需要同等速度的防禦加速。目前我們看到全自動化攻擊已成可能，但核心防禦循環與行為的完全自動化尚未有實作證明。

We believe it’s vital at this moment to begin accelerating defense and finding ways to automate SDLC, in the modern parlance, so incident response, vulnerability detection, vulnerability patching. There’s some things that stand out acutely as challenges for the industry to begin tackling with high urgency. So continuous agentic red teaming is one of them. As you can see from this incident, agents are quite good at finding zero-day attacks in the infrastructure of companies. The question that’s now going to be posed is whether companies are able to invest sufficient model intelligence and effort in finding and remediating their vulnerabilities before someone else that’s a threat actor does it for you.我們認為現在至關重要地是加速防禦並尋找自動化 SDLC 的方法，例如事件響應、漏洞檢測與修補。其中一些挑戰急需產業解決，例如持續性的代理紅隊演習。如本次事件所示，代理程式擅長在公司基礎設施中發現零日漏洞。現在的問題在於，公司是否能在威脅者出手前，投入足夠的模型智能與努力來發現並修復漏洞。

This style of operating will be different now, but ultimately we need to invest in having AI agent red teaming that enables defenders to find and remediate vulnerabilities before attackers do. But automating these defensive loops is not trivial, and so if we do this partially, we will fail to meet the scalability of the offensive acceleration that we have just seen. So for example, if we automate vulnerability finding without automating patching, we will shift the bottleneck from vulnerabilities to patching to remediation, and we will simply drown or inundate human software engineers in new vulnerabilities to fix and patch. This is not a problem whose end state we can solve partially. We will need to take these core defensive loops and fully automate them, which will require conversations with infrastructure and product partners and reaching to a point where we can say, if a vulnerability is identified, not only can an agent identify that vulnerability, we can have an agent propose a patch, we can have automated infrastructure to roll out a change with that patch, and roll it back if there is an availability incident or outage. That loop needs to be fully automated in its end state. Of course, we want to automate as progressively and iteratively quickly as we can, but if we don’t reach that end state, then we will be comparing a core defensive loop of fixing vulnerabilities that is a human in the loop and is much slower and less scalable, with an offensive loop that is fully automated, and that is an unsustainable position for this industry to be in.現在的運作方式將有所不同，但最終我們需要投資於 AI agent 紅隊演習，讓防禦者能在攻擊者之前發現並修復漏洞。然而，自動化這些防禦迴圈並不簡單；若僅部分實現，將無法應對攻擊加速的規模化挑戰。例如，若僅自動化漏洞發現而未自動化修補，瓶頸將從漏洞轉移到修補與修復，導致軟體工程師被海量漏洞淹沒。這不是一個能部分解決的問題。我們必須將核心防禦迴圈完全自動化，這需要與基礎設施和產品夥伴協作，達到漏洞被識別後，不僅能由 agent 識別，還能由 agent 提出修補方案，並透過自動化基礎設施部署修補程式，且在發生可用性事故或停機時能自動回滾。這個迴圈在最終狀態必須完全自動化。當然，我們希望盡快逐步迭代地實現自動化，但若無法達到該最終狀態，我們將面臨防禦端依賴人工且緩慢、難以規模化的困境，而攻擊端已完全自動化，這對產業而言是不可持續的局面。

This situation is obviously completely novel; Dalton is arguing that it will become commonplace. Some of the issues he is raising, however, are not novel at all.這種情況顯然完全是全新的；Dalton 認為這將變得普遍。然而，他提出的一些問題一點也不新穎。

Go back to the concept of a bug bounty program. Software is incredibly complicated and brittle and built on a foundation of code that, if you dig deep enough, often goes back decades; there is so much code and so many dependencies that no company, no matter how security conscious they are, could ever ensure it is perfect. This reality is what creates the opportunity for black hat hackers: a bad actor can probe software, find bugs, and exploit them; the most effective defensive preparation is to do the exact same thing. That could entail regular penetration testing (pen testing) by a “red-team”, or simply paying the would-be bad actors to be on your side.回顧漏洞賞金計畫的概念。軟體極其複雜且脆弱，其基礎程式碼往往可追溯至數十年前；程式碼與依賴項數量之多，使得任何公司無論多重視安全，都無法確保其完美。這為黑帽駭客創造了機會：惡意行為者可探測軟體、尋找漏洞並利用之。最有效的防禦準備就是採取相同做法，例如由「紅隊」定期進行滲透測試，或直接支付費用讓潛在攻擊者站在防禦方。

It’s worth noting, however, that this approach to defense only arose after offensive black hat hackers had been breaking into systems for years. The problem wasn’t that they were uniquely capable, but rather that they were uniquely incentivized: breaking into systems was good business; companies hosting those systems, on the other hand, were insufficiently incentivized to invest in defense. Spending money on security is well-spent if nothing happens, and unfortunately that is a difficult budget line item to argue for when it only moves the needle on costs, not revenue.但值得注意的是，這種防禦方式是在黑帽駭客入侵系統多年後才出現的。問題不在於駭客的能力，而在於誘因：入侵系統是有利可圖的生意，而託管系統的企業則缺乏投資防禦的誘因。如果安全投資後沒事發生，這筆預算在僅能增加成本而非增加營收時，很難被爭取到。

This is where Dalton’s concerns echo past industry indifference. What the Hugging Face incident showed is that agents, with their ability to scale attacks with compute and autonomously develop exploits for vulnerabilities they find, are a threat today, but that companies are not investing in the capabilities necessary to defend themselves.這正是 Dalton 的擔憂與過去產業的冷漠相呼應。Hugging Face 事件顯示，AI 代理能利用運算力擴大攻擊規模並自主開發漏洞利用工具，已成今日之威脅，但企業尚未投資於必要的防禦能力。

There is good news, however: in this new agent-defined security landscape, defense should be at an advantage in a way it wasn’t in the hacker era. It used to be that the best defenders could do is mimic the tactics of the offense, and/or pay them off, because preemptively finding all of the bugs was not viable. However, that is changing: it actually is — or soon will be — possible to meticulously go over an entire code base, including all of its dependencies, and look for bugs and patch them. Notice the structural advantage available to defenders: they actually have the code in question; offensive agents need to probe and discover vulnerabilities without the same advantage.然而，有個好消息：在 AI 代理定義的新安全格局中，防禦方應比駭客時代更具優勢。過去防禦者只能模仿攻擊者的策略或支付費用，因為預先找出所有漏洞並不可行。但現在情況正在改變：仔細檢查整個程式碼庫及其所有依賴項並修補漏洞已成為可能。防禦者擁有程式碼，而攻擊者必須在缺乏此優勢的情況下探測漏洞。

What was illuminating about Dalton’s overview, however, was the implication embedded in his explanation of why this isn’t currently enough. Specifically, the expected value for a hacker’s automated attack is always positive. If the offensive agent finds a vulnerability and creates an exploit, and that exploit fails or is itself buggy, then nothing has changed about the status quo: the exploit doesn’t work (or, perversely, makes the original vulnerability larger by virtue of its own bugs); if the agent executes the exploit perfectly, meanwhile, the attacker has gained access to the system. The attack only needs to work once for the entire endeavor to have a positive payoff.Dalton 的概述中啟發人心之處在於其解釋中隱含的意義。具體來說，駭客的自動化攻擊期望值始終為正。如果攻擊代理程式發現漏洞並建立漏洞利用程式，且該程式失敗或有 Bug，則現狀未變：漏洞利用失敗（或因 Bug 導致漏洞更大）；而若代理程式完美執行，攻擊者即可進入系統。攻擊只要成功一次，整個嘗試就具有正面回報。

The challenge for the defender, on the other hand, is that they need to keep the software in question working correctly, and not make the situation worse. This means that any automation has a negative expected value: successful automated vulnerability discovery and patching preserves the status quo, i.e. the software is not hacked. However, any unsuccessful patches make the situation worse, either by breaking the software or by introducing new vulnerabilities. The agent only needs to fail once for the entire endeavor to have a negative payoff.另一方面，防禦者的挑戰在於必須確保軟體正常運作且不惡化情況。這意味著任何自動化都有負的期望值：成功的自動化漏洞發現與修補僅能維持現狀（即軟體未被駭），但任何失敗的修補都會使情況惡化，例如導致軟體崩潰或引入新漏洞。代理程式只要失敗一次，整個嘗試就具有負面回報。

This is the dynamic that leads to the exact situation Dalton describes, where offensive actors are fully automated while defensive systems, even if they use AI, will be incentivized to keep a human in the loop, and no human in the loop will be able to keep up with fully automated agents. Truly effective defense will mean truly trusting agents to act autonomously, but most companies won’t do that until they are forced to by regular and unremitting hacks by fully autonomous attackers.這種動態導致了 Dalton 描述的情況：攻擊者完全自動化，而防禦系統即使使用 AI，也會傾向於保留人工審核。然而，人工審核無法跟上完全自動化的代理程式。真正有效的防禦意味著必須完全信任代理程式自主行動，但大多數公司直到被自動化攻擊者頻繁且持續地駭入後，才會被迫這樣做。

Why AI Diffusion Takes Time為什麼 AI 普及需要時間

Over the weekend David Senra released a new podcast episode with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, where Altman admitted he had been wrong about the speed of AI diffusion into the broader economy:週末時，David Senra 發布了與 OpenAI CEO Sam Altman 的新播客集數，Altman 在其中承認他之前低估了 AI 普及到整體經濟中的速度：

I love startups. I think startups are the coolest thing in the economy and I’ve spent my career trying to like really understand startups, and I thought when we got to GPT-4, which was back in 2023, I think, that very quickly after that, there was going to be much more disruption in software businesses being up for grabs right away than turned out to be.我熱愛新創公司。我認為新創是經濟體系中最酷的事，我職業生涯一直試圖深入了解新創。我原以為 2023 年 GPT-4 出現後，軟體業務的顛覆與機會將會比實際情況來得更快。

I was wrong about a few things, but one of them in terms of the speed, one of them is the economy just has so much inertia. People keep doing the same things they’re doing. They keep buying from the same, you know, company. They keep sort of wanting to use their tools in the same way. I think it’s actually a positive in many ways and it’s going to make this big transition in front of us go smoother and slower. I’m grateful for it. But I think it means we’ve all been too ambitious on timelines even with this incredible technology. I think AI is one of the most incredible technologies humanity has ever invented. Society and the economy will adapt more slowly.我在某些方面想錯了，特別是速度。經濟具有極強的慣性，人們傾向於維持原有的習慣、購買相同的公司產品並使用相同的工具。我認為這在許多方面是好事，能讓未來的轉型更平穩緩慢。雖然 AI 是人類發明最驚人的技術之一，但社會與經濟的適應速度會較慢，這意味著我們對時間表的預期過於激進。

I of course think Altman is right about AI taking longer to sweep the economy generally and software specifically; I’ve been pushing back on these timelines all along. I also think his answer is incomplete in its explanation as to why, and Dalton’s warning about the mismatch between offensive agents and defensive preparations explains it.我當然認為 Altman 關於 AI 普及化至整體經濟與軟體業的時程較長之看法正確；我一直以來都對這些時間表持保留意見。但我認為他的解釋並不完整，而 Dalton 關於攻擊性代理與防禦準備之間失衡的警告正好解釋了原因。

First, GPT-4 was an incredible breakthrough; it’s also a model that wasn’t remotely capable enough to actually displace real world software. It didn’t even have the ability to reason, which is the key breakthrough that has unlocked the capabilities that Dalton described.首先，GPT-4 是一次驚人的突破；但它仍不足以取代現實世界的軟體。它甚至缺乏推理能力，而這正是解鎖 Dalton 所述能力的關鍵突破。

Second, what is meant by a model not being capable enough is that it makes mistakes and can’t be trusted. People can and were rightly awed by what AI can do, but the decision about actually implementing AI isn’t made according to what can be done, but about whether or not critical mistakes can be avoided.其次，所謂模型能力不足，是指它會犯錯且不可信。人們理所當然地對 AI 的能力感到驚嘆，但決定是否實際導入 AI，並非取決於它能做什麼，而是在於能否避免關鍵性錯誤。

In other words, incumbent companies are inevitably going to approach AI with a bias towards a negative expected value framing: AI ideally will make their existing operations more productive; what they are most concerned about is AI making a mistake that blows up in their faces. What that means is humans will continue to be in the loop, which will always be a bottleneck.換句話說，既有公司必然會以負面預期值的框架來看待 AI：理想情況下 AI 能提高生產力，但他們最擔心的是 AI 犯錯導致災難。這意味著人類將繼續參與其中，而這將永遠是瓶頸。

This will, in the long run, be a mistake, just like it will be a mistake for companies to keep a human in the loop when it comes to agentic defense. As Dalton noted, the only way to defend yourself against fully automated attacks is to fully automate your defense, but it will take a while for defenders to accept the trade-offs that entails. And, by the same token, the companies that win in their category will be truly driven by AI, instead of simply AI as productivity enhancer. The great irony in Altman’s answer is that he actually identified how these companies will arise: they won’t be incumbent companies overhauling how they work; rather, the true AI-native companies will be startups.長期來看，這將是一個錯誤，就像在代理防禦中保留人工干預同樣是錯誤的。如 Dalton 所言，對抗全自動攻擊的唯一方法是全自動化防禦，但防禦者需要時間接受其權衡。同樣地，類別領先的公司將由 AI 真正驅動，而非僅將 AI 作為生產力工具。Altman 回答中的諷刺之處在於，他指出了這些公司的誕生方式：它們不會是轉型中的既有公司，而將是 AI 原生新創公司。

Sustaining Versus Disruptive Innovation持續性創新 vs 顛覆性創新

Back in 2023, shortly after ChatGPT came out, I put forward the question in AI and the Big Five as to whether AI would be a sustaining or disruptive innovation:2023年ChatGPT推出後不久，我在《AI 與五大科技巨頭》中提出了一個問題：AI 會是持續性創新還是顛覆性創新？

The story of 2022 was the emergence of AI, first with image generation models, including DALL-E, MidJourney, and the open source Stable Diffusion, and then ChatGPT, the first text-generation model to break through in a major way. It seems clear to me that this is a new epoch in technology. To determine how that epoch might develop, though, it is useful to look back 26 years to one of the most famous strategy books of all time: Clayton Christensen’s The Innovator’s Dilemma, particularly this passage on the different kinds of innovations:2022年是 AI 的崛起之年，首先是 DALL-E、MidJourney 和開源的 Stable Diffusion 等圖像生成模型，接著是首個取得重大突破的文本生成模型 ChatGPT。對我而言，這顯然是一個新的技術時代。然而，要判斷這個時代如何發展，回顧 26 年前最著名的策略書籍之一——Clayton Christensen 的《創新者的窘境》，尤其是關於不同創新類型的段落會很有幫助：

Most new technologies foster improved product performance. I call these sustaining technologies. Some sustaining technologies can be discontinuous or radical in character, while others are of an incremental nature. What all sustaining technologies have in common is that they improve the performance of established products, along the dimensions of performance that mainstream customers in major markets have historically valued. Most technological advances in a given industry are sustaining in character…大多數新技術會提升產品性能，我稱之為「持續性技術」。有些持續性技術具有不連續或激進的特性，有些則是漸進式的。所有持續性技術的共同點在於，它們能沿著主流市場客戶歷來重視的性能維度，提升既有產品的性能。特定產業中大多數的技術進步都屬於持續性技術。

Disruptive technologies bring to a market a very different value proposition than had been available previously. Generally, disruptive technologies underperform established products in mainstream markets. But they have other features that a few fringe (and generally new) customers value. Products based on disruptive technologies are typically cheaper, simpler, smaller, and, frequently, more convenient to use.顛覆性技術為市場帶來了與以往截然不同的價值主張。通常，顛覆性技術在主流市場中表現不如既有產品。但它們具有少數邊緣（且通常是新）客戶所重視的其他特徵。基於顛覆性技術的產品通常更便宜、更簡單、更小，且通常更方便使用。

It seems easy to look backwards and determine if an innovation was sustaining or disruptive by looking at how incumbent companies fared after that innovation came to market: if the innovation was sustaining, then incumbent companies became stronger; if it was disruptive then presumably startups captured most of the value.回頭看創新是持續性或顛覆性的似乎很容易：若持續性，現有公司會更強；若顛覆性，則新創公司通常會獲取大部分價值。

I think it speaks to the incredible capability of AI that it is setting up to be both. There are massive productivity benefits from AI right now; for most knowledge workers leveraging those benefits is a matter of agency, but for software developers in particular it is increasingly a matter of necessity.我認為 AI 的強大能力使其能兼具兩者。目前 AI 帶來巨大的生產力提升；對多數知識工作者而言，這取決於主動性，但對軟體開發者來說，這已逐漸成為必然。

That distinction between agency and necessity, however, is an important one: if leveraging a technology depends on humans figuring it out, then penetration will be limited by human creativity and risk taking. Those limits will be very strong in any sort of established company, because the risk calculus will be biased towards avoiding the downsides. Those calculations will make AI sustaining, but nothing more.然而，主動性與必然性的區別至關重要：若技術應用取決於人類的探索，則普及率將受限於創意與冒險精神。在成熟企業中，風險考量往往傾向於規避損失，這將使 AI 僅能維持現狀，而無法突破。

Human creativity and risk taking in the form of a startup, however, operates with a completely different risk profile. For startups the base case is failure; that means that anything that makes success more likely has positive expected value, which is to say that truly leaning into AI will be nothing but upside. Or, to put it another way, it is startups who will be the offensive hackers with nothing to lose by automating everything; it is the incumbents they will be attacking who will be so worried about losing what they have that they will keep humans in the wrong loop for too long.然而，以新創形式呈現的人類創造力與風險承擔，其風險概況完全不同。對新創而言，失敗是常態；這意味著任何能增加成功機率的因素都具有正向預期價值，也就是說，全力投入 AI 將只有好處。換句話說，新創公司將成為進攻的駭客，透過自動化一切且毫無損失，來攻擊那些因擔心失去既有優勢而讓人類留在錯誤流程中太久的既有企業。

Same tools, different incentives, and, in the very long run, very different outcomes.相同的工具，不同的誘因，長遠來看，結果截然不同。
